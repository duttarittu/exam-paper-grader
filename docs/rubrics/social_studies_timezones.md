# Rubric — "Why do we have different time zones?"

Subject: Social Studies. Max marks: 6.

This is a worked example input for Step 2 (descriptive grading with
partial credit). A rubric like this is required input for every
descriptive question — the agent never invents grading criteria on its
own.

## Criteria (must sum to max marks)

1. **Mentions the Earth rotates/spins on its own axis** — 2 marks
   - Full 2: clearly states the Earth spins/rotates on its axis.
   - Partial 1: vaguely implies motion/rotation without naming it, or
     only says the Earth is round (roundness alone is not the cause).
   - 0: no mention of rotation at all.

2. **Explains that rotation causes day on one side and night on the
   opposite side at the same time** — 2 marks
   - Full 2: clearly explains one side faces the sun (day) while the
     opposite side does not (night), at the same time.
   - Partial 1: mentions day/night exist but doesn't connect it to
     "opposite sides, same time."
   - 0: no explanation of the day/night mechanism.

3. **Connects this to why different countries set different clock
   times (time zones follow the sun's position for that region)** — 2
   marks
   - Full 2: explicitly says different places set their clocks
     according to whether the sun is up for them.
   - Partial 1: says countries have different times but doesn't explain
     why (no link back to the sun/rotation).
   - 0: no mention of time zones/countries differing at all.

## Model (best possible) answer, for reference

"The Earth rotates on its own axis once every 24 hours. Because it is
round, only the half facing the Sun experiences daylight at any given
moment, while the opposite half experiences night. As the Earth keeps
rotating, different regions face the Sun at different times. To keep
local clocks roughly matched with the Sun's position (so that around
noon the Sun is near its highest point), the world is divided into time
zones, each set to a different local time."

Grading rule for this project: full marks (6/6) only for an answer this
complete; partial marks per criterion otherwise; 0 for no relevant
content. The model never awards a self-reported single total — points
are assigned per criterion and summed in code.
